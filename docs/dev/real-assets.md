# Handing real art and music to the pipeline

Everything the game draws and plays today is a **placeholder generated by this repository** — pixel
maps and procedural generators for the sprites (`assets/source/`, `scripts/assets/procedural/`),
ZzFX-style parameter sets and chip songs for the audio (`content/audio/`), and, since M3-03,
agent-written translations for the string tables (`content/strings/`). That was a deliberate rule
from the start (plan §1.5: "placeholder assets are generated by committed scripts or code"), and it
means every one of them can be replaced **one at a time, by name, without touching a line of
code**.

This page is the hand-off for the **v2 remaster** (640×360, [`plan_resolution.md`](../../plan_resolution.md)):
what an artist, a model or a composer produces, where it goes, and what the pipeline does with it —
and, for AI-assisted art, the **ingest tool** (`art-prompts/ingest/`, plan step R3-02) that turns a
generated image into a sprite source with its provenance. Its companions:

- [art-bible-v2.md](art-bible-v2.md) — **the rules for the art itself**: sizes and frame counts per
  category, outline, light, palettes, alpha, dither, and the key colour and ingest rules (§8) this
  tool implements;
- [art-provenance.md](art-provenance.md) — which models' output may ship, and what every shipped
  AI-assisted image records;
- [asset-pipeline.md](asset-pipeline.md) (how the atlas is built) and [audio.md](audio.md) (how
  sound reaches the speakers).

## The rules that do not change

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Original work only** — never a Konami or Taito name, sprite, sound or look-alike; no prompt names a game, studio, artist or character | `shmup_feat.md` §26; `test/integration/art-prompt-names.test.ts`; the store review checks it too |
| Art is drawn at the **native 640×360 density** — the 640×336 playfield between two 12-px HUD bars, ×1.5 world sizes (the ship 24×14, 12-px tiles). Never upscale v1 art, never mix pixel sizes ("mixels") | art bible §1, §3.1. *The code still runs at 384×216 until R2-01; v1's art stays on `v1.x`* |
| Sprites are **indexed-looking pixel art with hard edges and binary alpha**; the renderer samples nearest and never rotates or scales a sprite (laser beams excepted, bible §3.2) | decisions D24, D30; art bible §3.2, §3.6 |
| An atlas page is at most **2048 × 2048** and the whole `dist/` at most **8 MB** | `apps/tizen/scripts/check-bundle.mjs`; R3-04 sets the v2 budgets |
| Music loops **sample-exactly**; nothing is decoded mid-stage | decision D22, `shmup_tech.md` §2.4 |
| A replacement keeps the **name** of what it replaces | that is what makes it a drop-in |
| **Every override PNG is traced**: a `<name>.art.json` provenance record, or a `LICENSES.md` entry | `test/scripts/assets/provenance.test.ts`; [art-provenance.md](art-provenance.md) §6 |

## Sprites: how the pipeline reads a real-art PNG

A sprite is known by its path below `assets/source/sprites/`: `ships/kestrel`,
`bosses/bulwark-hull`, `enemies/sentry`. The content refers to it by that name, so real art that
keeps the name replaces the placeholder everywhere at once. `assets/source/sprites/<name>.sprite.json`
(or a generator in `scripts/assets/procedural/`) holds the placeholder's frame size, frame count,
anchor and animations; a `<name>.png` next to it **overrides** its pixels
(`scripts/assets/sprite-source.mjs`):

- **Without a sidecar the whole PNG is frame 0.** A horizontal strip is *not* cut into frames by
  itself — a multi-frame sprite needs a sidecar.
- **With an Aseprite JSON sidecar** (`<name>.json`, hash or array format — `File → Export Sprite
  Sheet` with "JSON Data", or the one the ingest tool writes) sidecar frame `i` becomes frame `i`;
  `meta.frameTags` become animations; **the first slice's pivot becomes the anchor and wins over the
  code-defined anchor** (since R3-02 — before, a ×1.5 PNG kept its ×1 anchor). Without a pivot the
  code anchor stays.
- Frame `i` of the PNG replaces frame `i` of the code-defined sprite; frames the PNG lacks keep
  their code-defined pixels; extra frames are appended. A PNG with no code-defined counterpart adds
  a new sprite — which shifts the sorted sprite ids, so the goldens move (a deliberate re-bless).
- Ignored next to the PNG: `README.md`, `LICENSES.md`, `.gitkeep`, editor files such as
  `*.aseprite`, and the ingest tool's `<name>.recipe.json`, `<name>.art.json` and
  `<name>.edit.png`.

**What the pipeline derives, and what it does not:**

| Variant | From the art? |
|---|---|
| `<name>@flash` (the hit flash, D30) | **yes** — the white silhouette of the override's opaque pixels |
| `<ship>@p2`, `hud/life@p2` (player 2) | **yes**, but by swapping red and blue (`scripts/assets/coop.mjs`), which is unpredictable on full-colour art — R3-03 replaces it with a palette map in the ship's recipe |
| `<bullet>@deuteranopia` and the other colour-blind variants | **no** — `scripts/assets/procedural/palettes.mjs` redraws them from its own shapes. A real-art bullet, beam or bend segment needs its `@<palette>` variants overridden too, frame for frame, or `pnpm content:check` fails. Bullets stay procedural (bible §6) |

## Two ways in

| | AI-assisted (ingest tool) | Hand-drawn (Aseprite) |
|---|---|---|
| Who | backdrops, boss bodies, large enemies, set pieces; concepts for mid-size enemies, shields, FX (bible §6) | the ship, items, capsules, tiles, HUD, font, and the cleanup of everything else |
| Produces | `<name>.recipe.json` → `<name>.png` + `<name>.json` + `<name>.art.json` (+ your `<name>.edit.png`) | `<name>.png` + `<name>.json` (export with a slice for the pivot) |
| Provenance | written by the tool | a hand-written `<name>.art.json` with `"origin": "hand"`, or an entry in a `LICENSES.md` |

**Where it goes.** After R2-01 (the switch to 640×360): `assets/source/sprites/<name>.*`. **Until
R2-01 the output is staged** in `art-prompts/v2/ingested/<name>.*` (plan R3-02), so the 384×216
atlas and the goldens do not change; the files move into `assets/source/sprites/` with R2-01 or
the first R4 step after it. The tool, `provenance.test.ts` and CI treat both trees alike.

## The ingest tool (`art-prompts/ingest/`)

A standalone tool outside the pnpm workspace, like `tools/*`: its own `package.json`, npm lockfile
and `node:test` suite. Its only dependencies are two pure-JavaScript image codecs (`pngjs`,
`jpeg-js`).

```sh
cd art-prompts/ingest && npm ci                      # once
node art-prompts/ingest/ingest.mjs                   # every recipe under assets/source/sprites/ and art-prompts/v2/ingested/
node art-prompts/ingest/ingest.mjs art-prompts/v2/ingested/bosses/maw-hull.recipe.json
node art-prompts/ingest/ingest.mjs --check           # re-run in memory; exit 1 when a file on disk differs (CI)
cd art-prompts/ingest && npm test                    # node --check + the tests, on synthetic fixtures
```

| Option | Effect |
|---|---|
| `<recipe.json \| dir>…` | the recipes to run; a directory means every `*.recipe.json` below it. None: both trees above |
| `--check` | write nothing; compare the PNG and the sidecar byte for byte and the record without its `ingestedAt` date; exit 1 on any difference |
| `--out <dir>` | write `<dir>/<name>.*` instead of next to each recipe |
| `--root <dir>` | the root that source paths and recorded paths are relative to (default: the repository) |
| `--palettes <dir>` | named palettes (default `art-prompts/ingest/palettes/`) |
| `--models <file>` | the model table (default `art-prompts/ingest/models.json`) |
| `--date <YYYY-MM-DD>` | the record's `ingestedAt`; by default kept when nothing else in the record changed, else today (UTC) |
| `--report <file>` | also write the full report as JSON |
| `--strict` | warnings fail too |
| `-q`, `--quiet` | print only failures, warnings and the summary |

Exit codes: 0 fine, 1 a recipe failed (or `--check` found a difference, or `--strict` met a
warning), 2 bad usage. CI's `ingest` job runs `npm test` and `--check`.

### What it does (art bible §8)

1. **Crop or slice** each source image into frame regions (a crop box, a list of boxes, or a sheet
   grid read left to right, top to bottom).
2. **Key** each region: the key colour is **sampled from the region's own border** (the median of
   the border pixels that pass the green test `G ≥ 150, G − R ≥ 70, G − B ≥ 120` — the models
   deliver a yellowish `#3cf102`, not `#00ff00`), then a **4-connected flood fill from the
   border** through pixels that pass the test and lie within the tolerance of that key. Never a
   global key: a key-green part enclosed by the subject (the green capsule's glass) survives.
   **Holes** — background seen through the subject — are keyed with `holes: "auto"` (enclosed
   key regions of at least `minArea` source px) or explicit seeds. Then an optional **opening** and
   **island** cleanup, and the **despill**: subject pixels within `despill` source px of the key
   get `G` clamped to `max(R, B)`, which takes the green fringe off outlines and thin features.
   Translucent and additive art uses a **black key** instead: alpha (or additive light) comes from
   the brightness.
3. **Downscale** with an exact **area filter** straight to the frame size (fractional factors
   included; never up — a smaller region is an error), then **pixel-snap**: each pixel takes the
   real source colour nearest its box's average, never a blend of an outline and a highlight.
   **Binary alpha:** opaque when at least half of the box is subject.
4. **Quantise** all frames to one palette: the recipe's named or explicit palette, or a median cut
   of the art, within the bible's per-category colour cap (§4.3). Every colour but the recipe's
   own cycle ramps is pushed **≥ 3/255 (Chebyshev) from every reserved palette-cycle colour** —
   the `cycles` ramps of every stage in `content/stages/` plus the recipe's `reserved` (§4.4);
   cycle art keeps its ramp exactly. Ordered (Bayer) dither only where the bible allows it (§3.7).
5. **Outline:** the outermost ring of opaque pixels takes the outline colour (default: the
   palette's darkest colour) — inside the frame, so the size, the anchor and the hitbox never
   move. Tiles (`"edges": "closed"`) outline only edges that face transparency inside the tile.
6. **Hand-edit layer:** `<name>.edit.png` next to the recipe, the size of the output strip, is
   merged last, so re-ingesting never loses hand work. Opaque pixels paint; the erase colour
   (`#ff00ff` by default) makes a pixel transparent; transparent pixels change nothing;
   half-transparent ones are ignored and reported. Make it from a copy of `<name>.png`.
7. **Write** `<name>.png` (the frames side by side in one row), `<name>.json` and `<name>.art.json`.

**Determinism.** The same recipe, sources and edit layer give **the same bytes on every machine**:
the PNG has no ancillary chunk (no time, text or gamma), is written indexed (≤ 256 colours, fixed
palette order — Aseprite opens it with the exact palette) or 8-bit RGBA, and is compressed by the
tool's own DEFLATE (`lib/deflate.mjs`), not by Node's zlib. Every choice breaks ties by a fixed
rule. Only the record's `ingestedAt` reads the clock, and it is kept when nothing else changed.

### The report

Per recipe: frames and size, the downscale factor, the sampled key colours, the colour count,
and warnings for anything the bible asks a human to look at:

| Warning | Bible |
|---|---|
| a JPEG source (artefacts blur edges) | §8 |
| the subject touches the region border (few key pixels on it — crop first) | §8 step 1 |
| a region stretched by more than 2 % (`fit: "stretch"` with the wrong aspect) | — |
| a palette colour moved away from a reserved ramp, and pixels still within 3/255 of one | §4.4 |
| more colours than the cap (an explicit palette or the edit layer) | §4.3 |
| an outline colour over luma 45, or black | §3.4 |
| a **backdrop layer over the lighting budget**: far — floor 16, median ≤ 64, 95th percentile ≤ 140; mid-far and mid — floor 16, ≤ 80, ≤ 160; more than 5 % above luma 200 | §4.1 |
| **mixels**: every run of equal pixels is even-length (a ×2 upscale) | §3.1 |
| a hole seed on the subject, a model missing from `models.json`, half-transparent edit pixels | — |

### The recipe (`<name>.recipe.json`, `shmup-ingest-recipe@1`)

The recipe lives where its output goes: `<root>/<name>.recipe.json` → `<name>.png` next to it.
Its `name` must match that location. Unknown fields are errors; every rule below is checked before
anything runs.

```jsonc
{
  "format": "shmup-ingest-recipe@1",
  "name": "bosses/maw-hull",              // the sprite it makes (or overrides)
  "category": "boss-body",                // sets the colour cap, the alpha, dither and outline rules
  "frame": { "width": 210, "height": 120 },
  "frameCount": 2,                        // optional check
  "sources": [{
    "image": "art-prompts/v2/winners/boss/galvanic-maw/a.png",   // relative to the root
    "grid": { "x": 0, "y": 0, "cellWidth": 630, "cellHeight": 360, "columns": 2, "rows": 1 }
    // or "crop": [x, y, w, h], or "frames": [[x, y, w, h], …]; none = the whole image
  }],
  "holes": "auto",                        // or "none" (default), or { "auto", "minArea", "seeds": [[x, y]] }
  "despill": 2,                           // source px; false = off
  "cleanup": { "open": 0, "minIsland": 40 },
  "palette": { "max": 32 },               // or { "name": "zone-b" }, or { "colors": ["#…"] }; "outline": "#…"
  "dither": "bayer4",                     // "off" (default), "bayer2", "bayer4", { "matrix", "strength" }
  "anchor": [105, 60],
  "animations": { "idle": { "from": 0, "to": 1, "direction": "pingpong" } },
  "provenance": { "reviewed": { "on": "2026-11-02", "by": "owner" } }
}
```

| Field | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `category` | — (required) | one of the table below |
| `frame` | — (required) | the target frame size, at most 2046² |
| `sources[].image` | — | PNG or JPEG (detected from the bytes); several sources add frames in order |
| `sources[].crop` / `frames` / `grid` | the whole image | `grid`: `cellWidth`, `cellHeight`, `columns`, `rows`; optional `x`, `y`, `gapX`, `gapY`, `count` |
| `fit`, `align` | `stretch`, `center` | `contain` scales uniformly and places the region `center`, `bottom` or `top` |
| `alpha` | `binary` | `translucent` / `additive` only for the categories and sprites bible §3.6 lists; never on a cycled layer |
| `key.mode` | `green` (binary), `black` (translucent, additive) | also `none` (opaque, e.g. a far layer) and `alpha` (the source's own alpha) |
| `key.color`, `key.tolerance`, `key.test`, `key.threshold` | `auto`, 64, the green test, 8 | `test: false` keys by distance only; `threshold` is the black key's floor |
| `holes`, `despill`, `cleanup` | `none`, 2, no opening / islands | green key only |
| `snap`, `coverage` | `true`, 0.5 | the pixel snap and the binary-alpha threshold |
| `palette` | adaptive, `max` = the category cap | `name` reads `<palettes>/<name>.json` (`{ "schema": "shmup-palette@1", "colors": […], "outline"? }`); a `max` over the cap warns |
| `dither` | `off` | errors where the bible forbids it |
| `outline` | per category (off for translucent / additive) | `true`, `false` or `{ "color", "edges": "open" \| "closed" }` |
| `cycles` | none | ramps this art is quantised to exactly: `{ "stage": "zone-b", "layer": "mid" }` (from `content/stages/`) or `{ "colors": […] }` |
| `reserved` | none | extra colours to keep ≥ 3/255 away from |
| `layer` | — | backdrops only, required: `far`, `mid-far`, `mid`, `near`, `foreground` (the lighting budget) |
| `anchor`, `animations` | none | written into the sidecar as the slice pivot and the frame tags |
| `edit.erase` | `#ff00ff` | the hand-edit layer's erase colour |
| `provenance` | from the candidate sidecar | recipe-wide, or per source: `model` (a `models.json` key, or `"none"` for a hand-made source), `prompt` `{ file, entry }`, `seed`, `sidecar` (a path, or `false`), `author`, `use`, `cleanup`, `reviewed` `{ on, by }`, `notes` |

| Category | Colour cap | Alpha | Dither | Outline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ship` | 24 | binary | no | yes |
| `option`, `item`, `player-shot` | 12 | binary | no | yes |
| `shield` | 12 | binary, translucent | no | yes |
| `point-item` | 6 | binary | no | yes |
| `bullet`, `laser` | 5 | binary | no | no |
| `particle` | 6 | binary, additive | no | no |
| `enemy` | 16 up to 30 px, 24 above | binary | no | yes |
| `boss-part` | 24 | binary | no | yes |
| `boss-body` | 32 | binary | from 96 px | yes |
| `set-piece`, `ui` | 32 | binary | no | yes |
| `fx` | 24 | binary, translucent, additive | no | yes |
| `glow` | 32 | additive | no | no |
| `backdrop` | 32 | binary, translucent | far, mid-far, mid layers | no |
| `tile` | 32 | binary | no | yes, closed edges |
| `scene` | 32 | binary | yes | no |

**Provenance without retyping.** When a source `foo.png` has a candidate sidecar `foo.json` from
`art-prompts/v2/gen-candidate.mjs`, the tool reads the model, the seed, the prompt as sent, the
negative, the library entry, the size, steps and guidance, the generation time and the edit
references from it. Recipe fields override it.

### The output

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `<name>.png` | the frame strip: frame `i` at `x = i · width`, one row |
| `<name>.json` | Aseprite array format: `frames[i].frame`, `meta.frameTags` from `animations`, `meta.slices[0]` with the `anchor` as its pivot (and so the anchor the pipeline uses) |
| `<name>.art.json` | the provenance record below |
| `<name>.recipe.json`, `<name>.edit.png` | your inputs, committed next to them |

### The provenance record (`<name>.art.json`, `shmup-art-provenance@1`)

The fields [art-provenance.md](art-provenance.md) §5 asks of every shipped AI-assisted asset. A
sprite can be cut from several images, so the per-image fields sit **per source**.

| Field | What |
|---|---|
| `sprite` | the sprite name (its location) |
| `origin` | `ai-assisted` (a source has a model) or `hand` |
| `use` | `pixels` or `reference` (§2's two columns) — recipe `provenance.use`, default `pixels` |
| `cleanup`, `handCleaned` | `none` · `light` · `heavy` · `redrawn` — the recipe's, or `light` when the edit layer changed pixels, else `none`; `handCleaned` = `cleanup ≠ none` (the plan's yes / no) |
| `reviewed` | `{ "on", "by" }` of the content review (look-alikes, logos, stray text — §6 rule 5), or `null` |
| `ingestedAt` | the date (the plan's "date"), `YYYY-MM-DD` |
| `author` | who made or cleaned it, or `null` |
| `sources[]` | `path`, `sha256`, `format`, `width`, `height`; `model` (the **served** model id, `null` for a hand-made source) and `modelKey` (the group key); `weights` (repo, revision, transformer override) and `licence` (an id of §2) from `models.json`; `prompt` `{ file, entry, text, negative }`; `seed`, `size`, `steps`, `guidance`, `generatedAt`; `reference` (an edit's input images: path, SHA-256 and, from their own candidate sidecar, the model and licence that made them — an edit of a FLUX image inherits FLUX's hold); `generator` (the candidate sidecar's path, hash, id, slot, mode, instruction) |
| `recipe` | `{ path, sha256 }` |
| `edit` | `{ path, sha256, painted, erased }`, or `null` |
| `output` | `png`, `sha256`, `sidecar`, `sidecarSha256`, `frames`, `frameWidth`, `frameHeight`, `colors`, `alpha` |
| `tool` | `{ "name": "shmup-ingest", "version" }`; `null` in a hand-written record |
| `notes` | free text |

`art-prompts/ingest/models.json` maps each model group key (`flux1`, `flux2`, `sd35`, `qwenvl`,
`qwenedit`, `wan22`, `ltx25`, `ltxvideo`) to its served id, weights, **licence id** and the
**verdicts** of art-provenance.md §2 (`ship` / `hold` per use). Records hold facts; the verdicts are
policy and stay in the table, joined by licence id — change a verdict there and in
art-provenance.md together. The validator and the tree check are
`art-prompts/ingest/lib/provenance.mjs` (dependency-free: `validateRecord`, `checkProvenanceTree`,
`loadModels`, `LICENCE_IDS`), which `test/scripts/assets/provenance.test.ts` and the R3-05 origin
report import rather than restate.

**`provenance.test.ts`** fails when an override PNG under `assets/source/sprites/` (or
`art-prompts/v2/ingested/`) has neither a valid record nor a `LICENSES.md` entry, when a record has
no PNG, when the PNG or its sidecar changed after ingest (the hashes no longer match — hand edits
go in the edit layer and a re-run), or when a recipe was never ingested.

**`LICENSES.md`** (for hand-drawn or third-party art without a record): a Markdown file in the
PNG's folder or any folder above it, up to `sprites/`, that names the PNG by its path relative to
that file (`kestrel.png`, `ships/kestrel.png`) or by its sprite name in backticks
(`` `ships/kestrel` ``), with the author and the licence.

### Ingesting one winner, step by step

1. **Check that it may ship** ([art-provenance.md](art-provenance.md) §6) — today nothing made by
   FLUX ships, as pixels or as a reference. `"use": "reference"` records a design reference.
2. **Read the bible's entry** for the category: the frame size and count (§5), the colour cap, the
   alpha recipe.
3. **Write the recipe** next to where the output goes; the crop removes what keying cannot
   (ground strips, letterbox bars, darkened edges — bible §8 step 1).
4. **Run** the tool on it and read the report; adjust the crop, `holes`, `cleanup` or the palette
   and re-run until the warnings are the ones you accept.
5. **Clean up by hand** in `<name>.edit.png` (copy `<name>.png`, paint, erase with `#ff00ff`),
   re-run, and set `provenance.cleanup` if the work is `heavy` or the AI image was only a reference
   (`redrawn`). Record the content review in `provenance.reviewed`.
6. **Commit** the recipe, the edit layer and the three outputs; `--check` must pass. Then
   `pnpm assets` and `pnpm content:check` (after R2-01, when the files live under
   `assets/source/sprites/`).

## Music — Furnace / OpenMPT → `content/audio/music/`

The music content already models two paths, and M3-03 added a third:

| Field | What it is | Status |
|---|---|---|
| `song` | a chip song in JSON, rendered to PCM at load by the deterministic synth | what ships (the placeholder) |
| `file` | an OGG Vorbis file with sample-exact `loopStart` / `loopEnd` | the intended final format (decision D22) |
| `module` | a MOD / XM / IT / S3M module played live by libopenmpt | optional, off — see [`audio.md`](audio.md) |

**Compose in a tracker** (Furnace, OpenMPT, Deflemask) and **render to OGG**:

1. Write the track with an explicit loop point. Note the loop start and end **in samples at the
   render rate** — the tracker shows them, and guessing is how a loop seam becomes audible.
2. Render to a WAV, then encode to OGG Vorbis (`oggenc -q5`, mono or stereo; the loader decodes
   everything to 32 kHz, so nothing above that survives).
3. Ship the file with the app build so it sits at the relative URL the track names (the same rule
   the atlas pages follow — the TV loads from `file://`, so nothing is fetched from a server), and
   point the track at it:
   ```jsonc
   { "formatVersion": 1, "kind": "music", "id": "zone-a", "title": "AZURE VERGE", "cue": "Stage",
     "file": "audio/music/zone-a.ogg", "loopStart": 204800, "loopEnd": 1638400, "sampleRate": 44100 }
   ```
   Remove the `song` — a track carries **exactly one** of `song` or `file`.
4. `pnpm content:check` verifies the loop points; `pnpm audio:preview` writes WAVs of everything to
   `assets/generated/audio-preview/` so you can listen without the game.
5. Watch the memory: `@shmup/shell`'s `estimateMemory` budgets one zone's music set resident under
   100 MB. A long track at 32 kHz stereo is megabytes.

**Keeping the tracker module** alongside the OGG (`"module": "audio/music/zone-a.xm"`) costs
nothing and makes a future tracker build possible without re-exporting anything.

## Sound effects — jsfxr / sfxr → `content/audio/main.sfx.json`

Every cue is a parameter set the synth renders at boot. To replace one with a recorded sound, give
it `"file": "audio/sfx/<name>.ogg"` instead of `"params"`; everything else (priority, voice cap,
bus, panning) stays. Keep them short and pre-decoded — the TV never decodes mid-stage.

## Translations — `content/strings/`

The Spanish and Japanese tables were written by a build agent and **have not been reviewed by a
native speaker**. Replacing them is an edit of two JSON files and nothing else. The rules are in
[`content/strings/README.md`](../../content/strings/README.md): every id, only the glyphs the font
has, the `{0}` / `{1}` slots kept, and the fixed HUD codes left in English. A language that needs
glyphs the font lacks needs them added to the font source *and* to `UI_GLYPHS` in the same commit.

## Fonts — `assets/source/fonts/`

The font is hand-authored ASCII art, one row per line, in `assets/source/fonts/pixel6x8.font.json`
(6 × 8 today; v2's cell — 8×12 or 9×12 — is R4-03's to decide, and its 195 glyphs are redrawn by
hand, never by AI). A real font is the same file with better glyphs: keep the code points, and

## Checklist for a hand-off batch

- [ ] Names match what they replace, exactly; sizes and frame counts as the art bible's §5.
- [ ] AI-assisted art: may ship per [art-provenance.md](art-provenance.md) §6, its recipe and
      `.art.json` are committed, `node art-prompts/ingest/ingest.mjs --check` passes.
- [ ] Hand-drawn art: a hand-written `.art.json` or a `LICENSES.md` entry.
- [ ] `pnpm assets` is clean and the atlas still packs into its pages.
- [ ] `pnpm content:check` is clean (bullets: the colour-blind variants too).
- [ ] `pnpm test` is clean — `provenance.test.ts`, the atlas hash tests and the audio hash tests
      flag anything that changed that should not have.
- [ ] `pnpm build` is clean: the Tizen bundle check is what catches an atlas that grew past its
      budget.
- [ ] The golden replays are untouched — unless the batch adds a sprite name (sorted ids shift) or
      a frame count the simulation hashes (bible §5.2 †); then re-bless deliberately, with the reason.
