# Zones D and E: MAGMA DEEP, TEMPEST RIDGE and their bosses

How plan step **M2-12** replaced two more stub zones of the campaign with real ones — and how the
v2 run's **R5-04** (zone D, landed `927b137f`) and **R5-05** (zone E, landed `fe4cdae3`)
remastered them on the designs `docs/v2-run/designs/zone-d.md` and `zone-e.md`: **zone D, MAGMA

dive** to the game's deepest camera, a **branching destructible maze** whose hot-brick shortcuts
open alternate ambushes, a lava river, the captain **OBSIDIAN CLAW** and the core battleship

come from behind the ship, the mid-stage captain **THE STORM EYE**, the eye-of-the-storm dip and

### The dive

The remaster's dive is **two scroll stops with pans** (M2-07's `hold` key): the camera key at
x 5,400 holds 150 ticks and pans `yTo` **186** (124 px further than the view top needed — enough
to show the shelf the captain patrols), and the key at 8,700 holds 165 ticks and pans to
`yTo` **432**. That second number is the game's deepest camera, and it is zone D's own design
figure — not zone B's 400: the design asked for a view whose bottom lands **exactly** on the
map's last row, and the map is 64 rows (768 px) tall because of it — 432 + `PLAYFIELD_H` 336 =
768, so `rowsTall` 64 is derived from the 432 anchor, never the other way round. The surface
floor's heightfield ramps down into the pit; the cave segments have their floors and ceilings in
the deep rows. The design's dive was a **redesign**, not a rescale: v1's single 200-px stop (the
port's 300) became two steps, each a hold with a `yTicks` pan on the same tick count, and the
second half of the zone lives permanently at y 432 — the lava river, the obsidian ridge and the
boss arena are all deep-view sections now. Everything below uses what the stage runtime already
does with a pan:

- **Checkpoints.** The third and fourth (6,600 and 9,900) sit in the caves at their step's depth;
  the fifth (12,900) and sixth (16,200) are at y 432. `restartAt` replays the camera keys in live
  order, so a restart at 6,600 puts the camera back at y 186 — above it, 3,600 takes the ship
  back to the surface and the two steps happen again on the way.
- **Spawns** use the camera y of the moment: ground enemies, rocks and turrets placed after each
  step stand on the cave rock that the view shows then (the content test measures "ground enemies
  stand on rock" at the camera y each event fires at,
  [below](#the-content-checks-testintegrationcontent-zone-dtestts-content-zone-etestts)).
- **The ship** never leaves the view: the camera pan carries it down. The game's own starts — a
  practice start, the stage skip (`?skip=boss` starts deep in the caves), a checkpoint restart —
  spawn the ship where the camera is ([gotcha](#gotchas)).

### The destructible maze

The remaster's maze is v1's idea re-cut with a branch: **five wall pairs** stand across the deep
view between x 10,200 and 11,640 (map columns 850/880/910/940/970 and their +1 twins — each wall
two tiles, 24 px, thick), 48–60 px of open gap in each at a different height — **60, 48, 48, 48,
48** px — and the second and fourth pairs alternate `brick` (the M2-07 destructible tile, 4 hp)
with the new **`obsidian`** tile (6 hp, 15 points — darker, tougher). A checkpoint restart rolls
every broken brick and obsidian cell back (`DestructibleTerrain`'s rollback). Why 48 px: the
stage-runtime flyable-corridor floor (every column keeps ≥ 48 open px in the rows the camera
shows); the v1 72-px gaps became these.

Two **destructible shortcuts** branch the section (§4 of the design):

- the **`lower` channel** — a hot-brick plug two columns wide (`brick-hot`, 3 hp, columns
  1050/1051) fills a floor cut just past the maze's last wall; a trigger region along the cave
  floor (x 12,100–12,540, view rows 672–756) opens from x 12,020 until 12,600 and sets the flag
  `lower` while the ship rides it; the branch table then admits the **`low`** lines — slag-mine
  at 12,150, magma-geode at 12,250, slag-mine at 12,300 — and keeps the high route's bat and
  wisp pairs (`low-skip`, 12,200 and 12,420) out of the timeline: the deep lane trades a calm
  stream for the mine ambush and the geode hatch, and thirty-seven `vent` floor tiles smoke
  along it;
- the **`flank` channel** — a second region (x 14,750–15,050, view rows 480–552) opens at 14,700
  until 15,200; the flag `flank` admits the **`take`** line (the dense wisp stream at 14,900 and
  the zone's one **blue capsule** at 15,300) and skips the plain **`hold`** stream at 15,000:
  staying low along the ridge is the risk you take for the blue.

Over the back half of the maze a `lava-drip` ceiling (hazard tiles, rows 40–41, columns
11,500–13,740) bars the high lane, so the low lanes read as the maze's design, not a corner to
hide in. All 75 events of the authored table sit on the §5 rhythm; the four-way bot's branch
admit is a **harness** law, not content: MF8-c2 (`e6f4760b`) widened the set-piece premise so
zone D's two regions are flown like every other zone's set piece (the pin holds the two region
boxes above), and the same cure gave the dive its deepest-pan arm (246 ticks = 432 − 186).

#### The arms are destructible and the core goes first

The release-audit's **full-loadout corridor** gate changed this boss after S5b, and the change is
a law worth recording. A piercing player beam that overlaps an armour part *and* the core dies on
the armour's `Clink` — the beam never reaches what it passed through. Zone A's HB-01 learned it
with two shadows and fixed it by ordering hull before core; the bastion is the **third shadow**
case (armour, arms, core all on one line of fire), so the fix extends:

1. the **arms lose their `never`** — each segment takes hit points (the envelope 8 outer / 10 mid
   / **12 inner** hp per side, 300 / 450 / 600 points) and can be shot away, opening a corridor
   the way the bulwark's plates do; the turning sweep still shields the core between volleys of
   fire, but never permanently; and
2. the **core moves to part index 1** (right after the hull), the HALCYON BULWARK's own shape, so
   the entry-first `offerHit` scan can put a pierce *through* an arm into the core instead of
   dying on the arm's clink.

The outer-first hp shape (weakest farthest in) is the design's: shooting the chain from the tip is
the cheap route, and every segment killed shortens the shield's reach by 7.5 px.

#### The rotating shield arms are data

No engine feature was added for the arms. They are **boss parts**:

1. a `hub` part attached to the core (`parent: "core"`), with no hurtbox, no sprite and no hit
   points — it is never drawn or hit, it only turns;
2. **six armoured-turned-destructible arm parts** chained on the hub (each outer segment to the
   inner one), each hit as a **circle** (`radius`, M2-09 — a turned part cannot have a box); and
3. `boss.bastion` calls `spinPart` on the hub (the helper `spinHubs` spins every standing part
   whose parent is a core) with the phase's `spin`, and flips its sign every `reverseTicks`.

M2-09's part transforms place children by the parent's world angle, so the arm segments swing round
the core as the hub turns; their round sprite needs no heading frames. The spin is set when a phase
starts and the arms **keep the angle they have**, so a phase change never makes them jump — only the
speed (and sign) changes. The emitters are attached to the hull, not the hub, so they never turn.
Shots that meet a standing arm clink until it dies (hp, then a corridor); the core between the
sweeps takes the hits.

## The new behaviours (`core/behaviors`)

All three follow the coroutine rules of D29 (sleep until the next thing to do, whole-tick timers,
no allocation per wake). Defaults in brackets; the zone content overrides many of them. The v2
remasters **added no fourth behaviour**: the new types reuse existing scripts (`mine.burst` and
`hatch.spawner` for the slag-mine and magma-geode, `rock.fall` and `rammer.aimed` for the
ice-slab and lightbird, `captain.crab` / `captain.circler` for the two captains) and the two zone
bosses run the same scripts on the rosters' rebuilt part trees and hotter tunables.

### `rear.swoop`

The rear attacker. Spawned behind the view, it flies along its spawn row to the right at [`speed`
1.6 · 3/2 = 2.4] px/tick to view x [`turnX` 452 — a view coordinate, re-derived from v1's 280 at
R2-01's flip, not scaled], holds [`hold` 18] ticks, turns to the nearest player and —
half-way through the hold — fires an aimed [`ways` 1]-way of pink needles [`spread` 40] at
[`bulletSpeed` 1.3 · 3/2 = 1.95] (`ways` 0 = none), then leaves to the left at [`leaveSpeed`
1.4 · 3/2 = 2.1].

The motion is **one `Waypoint` mover** (approach to `turnX` on the row, hold, leave at
`-leaveSpeed`), so the script wakes exactly once: at the shot, `⌈|turnX − x| / speed⌉ + (hold >> 1)
+ 1` ticks after it starts. The distance is taken **either way**: a jumper spawned right of its
`turnX` flies back to it first and still fires half-way through its hold (the test round's fix — it
used to be timed as if it stood there already, so the shot fell inside the enemy's settle time and
was dropped). A `hold` below 1 is one tick, a `speed` of 0 is 1 px/tick, `ways` is floored. Like
every enemy shot, the needle goes through the primitives, so nothing fires off screen or before
`settleTicks` — every jumper needle starts inside the view (`zone-d-runtime.test.ts` / `zone-e-runtime.test.ts`, since R5-P.P5e folded the old `-runtime-edge` cases into them).

### `boss.bastion`

CINDER BASTION's script. Tunables (defaults, world units in `v1 · 3/2` form since R2-02's port):
[`trackSpeed` 0.35 · 3/2 = 0.525, `margin` 44 · 3/2 = 66] (follows the nearest player's
height, `margin` px inside the playfield), [`spin` 4] (binary units a tick — `ANGLE_UNITS` is
1,024 — rounded to whole units; negative turns the other way, 0 holds the arms) and [`reverseTicks`
0 = never] (the sign flips every so many whole ticks), [`laserTicks` 120, `firstLaser` 60] with
[`laserLength` `PLAYFIELD_W` (640), `laserWidth` 8 · 3/2 = 12, `telegraph` 45, `active` 50] (the
next standing gun fires an attached lane to the left; a `firstLaser` below 1 is one tick),
[`fireTicks` 100, `ways` 0 = none, `spread` 40, `bulletSpeed` 1.3 · 3/2 = 1.95] (aimed spreads of
red ovals from every standing core, through its
arms) and [`ring` 0 = none, `ringTicks` 150, `ringSpeed` 1 · 3/2 = 1.5] (rings of round purple
bullets from the cores, each turned half a gap from the last).

One script per phase that sleeps until the soonest of four timers (lane, spread, ring, reverse); a
timer that never runs is `NEVER_TICKS`, the fire timers go through `fireWait` (rank-scaled). Lanes
go to the standing guns in turn and skip a destroyed one, like `boss.bulwark`'s; with both emitters
gone there are no lanes.

### `boss.steed`

SQUALL STEED's script. Tunables (defaults, world units in `v1 · 3/2` form since R2-02's port): the
ellipse [`cx` `DEFAULT_BOSS_X` (`PLAYFIELD_W − 88 · WORLD_SCALE` = 508), `cy` `DEFAULT_BOSS_Y`
(`PLAYFIELD_CY` = 168), `rx` 8 · 3/2 = 12, `ry` 40 · 3/2 = 60] at [`bobSpeed`
3] units a tick (`api.orbit`; the boss should enter at `cx + rx`, `cy` — its home — so the bob
starts without a jump; `bobSpeed` 0 holds it), [`closedTicks` 150, `openTicks` 110] (the
`whenOpen` parts shut and open in turn, `setOpenAll`; below 1 = every tick), [`launchTicks` 45,
`minis` 2] (while open each standing core launches the `minion` — the first **10 ticks** after the
chest opens, then every `launchTicks`, at most `minis` per opening; 0 = none), [`fireTicks` 80,
`ways` 3, `spread` 44, `bulletSpeed` 1.3 · 3/2 = 1.95] (aimed spreads of pink ovals from every
standing gun — the snout; `ways` below 1 is a single shot), [`ring` 0 = none, `ringSpeed` 1 · 3/2
= 1.5] (a ring of round red bullets from each standing gun every time the chest **shuts**,
alternating by half a gap) and
[`gape` 0 = still] (the parts attached to a core — the lids — move `gape` px apart while it is open,
through M2-11's `setJaws`, measured from their rest offsets).

The bob's speed is **`bobSpeed`, not `speed`**: the content test's 3-px/tick check reads a boss
phase's `speed` tunable as a velocity in px/tick, and an orbit speed in binary units per tick is not
one. Every phase starts with the chest shut and the lids at rest (`setJaws(api, 0)`).

## Balance found by the bot

Deaths without god mode are reported, never asserted, but a zone should be survivable with four
directions. The v1 bot's runs changed the content in four places:

- **The maze walls got their gaps** ([above](#the-destructible-maze)).
- **Turrets and crawlers left the maze**: a turret passing under the ship fires straight up its
  column, which a lane-only dodger cannot leave in time between two walls. They now sit before and
  after it.
- **SQUALL STEED's bob** went from 40 px to 14–18 px (21–27 px on the ported world) ([above](#squall-steed-ss-05)).
- **CINDER BASTION's core** got 80 hit points (60 fell in 10 s once the bot had an Option; the
  remaster's is 96).

The remasters found their own, and they are worth the same honesty:

- **The bastion's arms became destructible and the core moved to index 1** — not from the lane
  bot but from the **release-audit's full-loadout gate**: the M2-18 audit line flies each boss
  with every weapon and the corridor rule died the fight on an armour `Clink` ([the ruling](#the-arms-are-destructible-and-the-core-goes-first)). The fix is also a design
  win: the sweep is now a shield you can whittle, segment by segment.
- **The storm eye's ring lost its hurtbox** ([above](#the-captain-the-storm-eye-te-01)): the same
  entry-first `offerHit` law that shielded the bastion's core would have made a concentric armour
  ring unkillable, so the ring is decor.
- **Ground rows stand on rock**: the remasters moved every floor/ceiling turret and the `ice-slab`
  rows to columns where the live heightfield really is rock at the camera y the event fires —
  D's lip moves (a cone 4,980 → 4,900, a rock 8,550 → 8,120, the deep pair 11,150 → 10,760 and
  11,200 → 10,800) and E's (turrets 1,886 → 1,908 and 5,006 → 4,672; seventeen ground rows on
  rock at S5) all read the generator's **first-bad-column** math, never a guess.
- **The bolt fold preserved a death**: the cheaper `tempest.bolt` fold was rejected precisely
  because it silently un-made the golden bot's scripted death ([above](#rear-attackers)) — a
  fixture's coverage outranks a tidier number.
- **The un-designed 4,290 row went** ([above](#zone-e-tempest-ridge)) — the event table audited
  against the design, and an extra v1 formation the design never listed was deleted, not ported.

hatch / mine / circler hosts of the new types are the wave's own already-guarded scripts — so all
three guards re-ran green at both lands unchanged.
