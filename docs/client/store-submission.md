> ## Nothing here has been submitted
>

> seller account, uploaded a build or started an alpha test. This is the checklist, and every item
> on it is yours.

The game itself is ready to be packaged: `pnpm --filter @shmup/tizen build` produces a checked
`dist/`, and the `tizen:package` / `tizen:install` / `tizen:run` scripts sign and deploy it (see
[`install-on-tv.md`](install-on-tv.md)). What is missing is the account side.

## The Tizen mandatory checklist (plan §8.6)

Run these on both monitors, on a **release** build (`pnpm --filter @shmup/tizen build`, not
`build:dev`):

- [ ] Launch to the title in **≤ 10 s**.
- [ ] No crash, no freeze, still responsive after a long session.
- [ ] Back on the title opens the exit confirmation; YES exits; NO stays.
- [ ] Back in a game pauses; Back on the pause menu resumes.
- [ ] Multitasking: Home during play pauses the game and silences the music; returning does not
      fast-forward.
- [ ] Resume from the Smart Hub works.
- [ ] Uninstalling deletes the user data (the save lives in `localStorage`, which Tizen removes
      with the app — confirm it).

## The account work

- [ ] **TV Seller Office account.** A *Public Seller* account can sell in the **US only**; other
      regions need a *Partner Seller* contract with Samsung.
- [ ] **App registration**: name, category, age rating, region, pricing.
- [ ] **Store assets.** `pnpm store:assets` writes placeholders into `assets/generated/store/`: the
      icon and four 1920 × 1080 screenshots captioned `PLACEHOLDER`, plus `listing.json` with the
      texts — drawn from the **v2** art since R4-05.E5 (the build moved off `artScale: 1`, closing
      the last RR-3 hold-out, so the placeholders wear the game's own remaster).
      **Check the sizes against the Seller Office's current requirements before you upload
      them** — they could not be verified from here, and they are written down in
      `scripts/store-assets.mjs` as an assumption, not as a fact. Replace the screenshots with real
      captures from a monitor.
- [ ] **Alpha test**: up to **50 DUIDs**. Both M7 monitors are in the 2020 model group, so they can
      be on the list; collect the DUIDs the same way you did for the distributor certificate.
- [ ] Upload the signed `.wgt`, run the alpha test, then submit for review.

## AI-content disclosure (every store)

The game was built by AI agents and its v2 art round works with generative AI (plan R3), so every
store gets a disclosure. What 2.0.0 **ships** includes AI-made image art — 231 of the atlas's 619
sprites are served from FLUX.2-dev real art under 142 committed `.art.json` provenance records
(`node scripts/art-origins.mjs` is the report), the rest drawn by the repo's committed programs and
pixel maps — and the agents wrote much of the text, the translations and the audio data. The owner
ruled the FLUX licence position on **2026-10-23** (R6-D4,
[`art-provenance.md`](../dev/art-provenance.md) §8 decision 1): this free open-source (MPL-2.0)
distribution is what the FLUX Non-Commercial licences allow, and a BFL commercial licence
(bfl.ai/licensing) is the condition on any **paid** distribution; re-run this section whenever the
shipped set moves. This
section covers all four stores, not only Samsung's; the other submission recipes are
[`webos.md`](webos.md), [`steam.md`](steam.md) and [`web-release.md`](web-release.md). Store
rules were read on **2026-10-17**; check them again at submission.

**What there is to disclose** (the facts behind every answer —
[`art-provenance.md`](../dev/art-provenance.md) has the details):

- **Art:** **231 of the 619 shipped sprites were made with the help of an AI image model.** They
  are FLUX.2-dev art — backdrops, bosses, ships, bullets and items — reduced to the game's
  resolution, keyed, quantised to its palettes and cleaned up by hand; 142 committed `.art.json`
  records trace every one to its sheet, prompt and seed, and the packer's derived `@flash`, `@p2`
  and `@palette` siblings carry the same origin (`node scripts/art-origins.mjs` is the report).
  The remaining 388 sprites — and the font — are drawn by the repo's own generators, pixel maps
  and font sources (itch.io's guideline says self-contained algorithms "don't require the use of
  generative AI tags"). The FLUX licence position was ruled by the owner on **2026-10-23** (open
  decision 1 there, R6-D4): the game's free open-source (MPL-2.0) distribution is what the FLUX
  Non-Commercial licences allow, and **a BFL commercial licence (bfl.ai/licensing) must be in
  place before any paid or commercial distribution** — the monetization row of
  [`outstanding-work.md`](outstanding-work.md) carries it. **Cleanup does not remove the duty to
  disclose** — Steam asks about content "created with the help of AI tools", itch.io about
  "materials produced by generative AI" (and, for asset pages, assets "comprised of generative AI
  (even if modified afterwards)") — so the draft text below describes what actually ships; re-run
  this section whenever the set moves.
- **Video:** none ships with the game. A trailer or store clip made with Wan2.2 or LTX-2.5 is
  AI-made content and LTX-2.5's licence adds its own duty: every LTX clip is **"expressly and
  intelligibly"** disclaimed as machine generated where it is shown (store video caption,
  trailer card).
- **Not only art:** the project's AI build agents wrote the Spanish and Japanese translations
  (never reviewed by a native speaker — [`real-assets.md`](../dev/real-assets.md)), and with them
  much of the in-game text, the placeholder music and sound data and most of the code — confirm
  which of it ships unchanged. Whatever does is AI-assisted content too: Steam's survey names
  "narrative, localization" and "sound".
- **Nothing is generated while the game runs.** The game has no AI at runtime (Steam's
  "Live-Generated" category does not apply).

**A draft text** for the description fields (adjust it to what actually ships):

> Shmup Cup's art was made in two ways: 231 of the game's 619 sprites were produced with the help
> of an AI image model (FLUX.2-dev), then reduced to the game's resolution, quantised to its
> palettes and cleaned up by hand — each carries a committed provenance record — and the rest of
> the art, every other sprite, tile and the font, is drawn by the game's own committed programs
> and pixel maps. The Spanish and Japanese translations, much of the in-game text and the
> placeholder music and sound data were written with the help of an AI assistant. [Trailer clips
> marked "machine generated" were made with AI video models.] The game does not generate any
> content while it runs.

### Steam — the Content Survey

- Steamworks → the app → **Content Survey**, section 3 "Generative Artificial Intelligence
  Content" ([Steamworks docs](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey)).
  Valve: "Efficiency gains through the use of these tools is not the focus of this section.
  Instead, it is concerned with the use of AI in creating content that ships with your game, and is
  consumed by players. This includes content such as artwork, sound, narrative, localization,
  etc."
- Answer **Pre-Generated** ("Any kind of content that ships with your game and is consumed by
  players that is created with the help of AI tools during development") and describe it — the
  draft above. Not **Live-Generated**.
- The description is **public**: store pages show it under "AI Generated Content Disclosure — The
  developers describe how their game uses AI Generated Content like this: …". Valve reviews the AI
  output like any other content, against the promise that the game holds nothing illegal or
  infringing.
- **Changing it later is not self-serve:** "Once your build and store page are approved for
  release, some questions in the survey cannot be edited without first contacting Steam Support."
  Answer for the final art, and update it (through Support) when an update adds AI content.

### itch.io — the AI disclosure field

- Project edit page → **AI generation disclosure**: "Yes" adds the
  [AI Generated](https://itch.io/games/tag-ai-generated) tag, and the kinds picked add
  **AI Generated Graphics**, **Sound**, **Text & Dialog** and **Code**
  ([itch.io announcement](https://itch.io/t/4309690/generative-ai-disclosure-tagging)). Use the
  form, not the tags by hand — itch says the automatic tags may be renamed.
- The [quality guidelines](https://itch.io/docs/creators/quality-guidelines): "We ask that you
  accurately tag your project if it contains materials produced by generative AI by utilizing the
  AI Disclosure section on your project's edit page." Self-contained algorithms (the repo's
  procedural art, game AI, procedural levels) "don't require the use of generative AI tags". Pick
  Graphics for the art, and Text & Dialog / Sound / Code for whatever of those ships AI-written.
- Projects that answer yes are listed on the "AI Assisted" browse page.
